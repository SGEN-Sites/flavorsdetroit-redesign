## Legend

- **PRESERVE** — kept as-is; content and function unchanged
- **IMPROVE** — same content and meaning, better structure, hierarchy or design
- **REPLACE** — superseded by a better structure (reason required)
- **REMOVE** — deliberately dropped (reason required)
- **ADD** — new section that did not exist on the source

## Imagery

Per-asset decisions are in `audit/image-inventory.json` (681 images: 26 KEEP, 1 REPLACE,
203 OPTIONAL, 451 REMOVE, each with its reason, by `tools/image-decisions.mjs`).

**From the source site:** the logo (resized only, never redrawn), the client's rainbow icon (the
favicon), and the client's own product photos (the Flower and Edibles cards, the floating bud on
`/shop/` and in the kinetic band, the Learn page). The social card is the logo on sign black with a
store-navy glow and the thin spectrum line (rebuilt 2026-11-09 for the black-first palette). The
black-and-gold promotion slides are not used, per the brief.

**The owner's store photos (2026-11-09, `tools/store-photos.py`, provenance in
`src/content/store-photos.json`):** four phone photos, upscaled 2x (fal Topaz High Fidelity V2, face
enhancement off) and colour graded (levels, edge-aware local contrast, a filmic curve, navy-leaning
shadows, a vignette, fine grain: tone and colour only). They replace the drawn "photo coming soon"
placeholders: the main counter and the green letter wall (home, who are we), the storefront (home
visit band), and on `/visit/` the sign, the front with the green cross, and the merch counter with
its blue LED underglow.

- **Storefront**, shot through a car windshield: the dashboard, the phone mount and a wiper streak
  were removed with AI fill (fal FLUX.1 [pro] Fill inside a mask) and the street below filled in;
  every pixel outside the mask is the photo, so the sign, the awning and every letter are as
  photographed (the R and the I hidden behind the utility pole stay hidden). Review fixes: lettering
  the fill invented on a small gas-station sign far left (that box restored from the photo), a curb
  spot re-filled with Bria Eraser, and (independent review) the wiper streak left over the small
  far-left signs taken out by subtracting its measured light, with no painting (a faint trace remains
  on the small gas-station sign).
- **Counter**: the six customers and staff in the photo were erased (fal Bria Eraser, object
  removal) for their privacy; the counter behind them is filled in; the rest is the photo. A first
  try with FLUX Fill was rejected because it painted new, invented people into the masks. A second
  eraser pass cleaned one spot far left. The independent review (every region compared with the
  original at 2–10x) then found a forearm remnant just below a mask and a dark post-like shape the
  second pass had invented; a third pass removed both, plus two wall prints that had been hidden
  behind people and came back garbled (now neutral and blurred). No trace of the six people remains.
- **Wall, merch**: no people; upscaled and graded only. Through the merch counter's far door a dark
  upright shape (perhaps someone's legs outside, not identifiable) stays as photographed: two ways of
  removing it left worse marks.
- **The home hero** (owner's choice, 2026-11-09: "make it look as if it was shot during the golden
  hour"): the storefront relit with fal Nano Banana Pro, 4 renders. One turned the blue tower grey
  and changed a building number, one repainted the black building tan and wrote mirrored lettering on
  a grey panel, one turned the tower pink; the fourth kept every real colour and is used. It garbled
  the hours poster in a door window (put back from the photo, registered to the relight and matched
  to its light), left faint lettering on the grey panel (smoothed out within the panel) and garbled
  the far-left street signs (cropped out). The sign, the awning, OPEN and the flag were compared at
  full size with the photo. To run behind the headline, the frame was then extended to the left with
  fal FLUX.1 [pro] Fill outpainting (the same street continuing at the same golden hour; the real
  frame is kept pixel for pixel), the new strip upscaled 4x with Topaz; four small signs the
  outpainting invented with pseudo-lettering had their faces smoothed blank, so no invented words
  remain. On the page that part is black and white and darkened behind the words.
- The edited files carry IPTC `DigitalSourceType = compositeWithTrainedAlgorithmicMedia` ("Edited
  using Generative AI") and `data-ai="edited"` on the `<img>`; the other two
  `algorithmicallyEnhanced`. The footer says the store photos are the store's own, retouched with AI
  where noted. Every alt text describes the pixels of its crop. The originals (two show the car and
  the customers) stay private in `assets/source/`, never published. Higgsfield was asked first, as
  requested, and refused all four renders (credit balance too low, not charged); spend on fal: about
  $1.34 at fal's list prices over 18 requests, every request id in the manifest (the four 4K relights
  are counted at the $0.15 list price; they may bill higher, so the fal dashboard has the exact sum).

**Generated (the cinematic pass, 2026-11-09):** seven scene stills shipped (nine were chosen; one was
retired after review and one at the owner's request, below), rendered with
fal-ai/flux-pro/v1.1-ultra (13 renders in all, $0.78 at the $0.06 list price, logged with prompt,
seed and request id in `assets/generated/generated.json` and `jobs-r1.json` / `jobs-r2.json`).
They are mood and product-category illustrations: none shows the store, its staff, its customers or
its signage, and none stands in for a store photo (the store slots hold the owner's own photos,
above). Every shipped file carries IPTC XMP `DigitalSourceType =
trainedAlgorithmicMedia` with a description naming the model; every `<img>` carries
`data-ai="generated"`; the footer says "scene images are AI illustrations, not photos of the store".
The provenance sidecar the templates read is `src/content/generated-images.json`
(`tools/process-gen.py`). Raw renders and rejected variants stay out of the public repo.
